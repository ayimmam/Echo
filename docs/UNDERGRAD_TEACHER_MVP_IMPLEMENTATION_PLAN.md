## 4. Feedback design recommendation

Start with educator-authored, evidence-linked cards and a deterministic selector. A pedagogy-tuned open model is unnecessary for this pilot. The uncertain input is the meaning of the sound, especially with a teacher-worn mic. Quiet can mean independent work, a dropout, or a room the transmitter does not pick up. Loudness can describe the captured signal and cannot establish pupil engagement. Generating fluent wording cannot resolve either ambiguity.

Each card needs an ID and version, eligible grades/subjects, goal, source reference, when it fits, when it does not fit, action, follow-up prompt and approved text in both languages. Have a relevant educator and fluent Amharic reviewer review meaning, examples and language suitability before release. Give teachers a way to say a suggestion did not fit and use that signal to improve the pack. A teacher who selects checking understanding might receive a card suggesting a quick response from each learner and a follow-up about what the responses revealed. A noisy recording alone never selects that card. The published teacher-coaching evidence motivates specific practice and follow-up. The pilot measures the usefulness of Echo’s own cards. [Kraft, Blazar and Hogan's coaching meta-analysis](https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654318759268).

If a later pilot needs context inferred from audio, treat it as a separate evidence task. Freeze a licensed model and its preprocessing, re-run through the actual AAC pipeline, and evaluate human-labeled held-out teachers/schools across English and Amharic, room layouts and K9 placements. Measure accuracy, abstention, robustness and phone resource cost. Document where a pattern is usable, show only observations supported by that evidence, and use the reviewed card library to turn eligible observations into suggestions. The supplied architecture's `UNKNOWN_ROLE`, coverage gates and shadow evaluation apply here. Shadow evaluation assesses candidates before display and is distinct from the proposed shadow-delete behavior.

## 5. Data, audit and usability contracts

### Research records

- Use the approved projection from the [v1](contracts/RESEARCH_SUMMARY_V1.md) or [v2](contracts/RESEARCH_SUMMARY_V2.md) contract for the enrolled study. Current v2 supports consented coarse grade, language and class-size bands, lesson count, recorded minutes, structured practice/follow-up, optional weekly rating and content version. Keep raw audio, excerpts, transcripts, test recordings, file paths and free-text notes out of both network destinations. Existing grant and record identifiers make the research data pseudonymous.
- Baseline as built — `Grant` has a one-to-many cascading relationship to `Summary`, summary uniqueness is `(grant, record_id)`, and `RateWindow`/`Maintenance` are operational tables. Current caps include 4 KB per request and 128 summaries/grant. Readiness fails when purge is stale. The dashboard suppresses totals and ratings separately for fewer than five eligible contributors and currently weights grants equally.
- Proposal path — Verify legacy rows and fixtures before adding C1–C5 in a reviewed migration. Test forward migration, rejection of each invalid row, rollback of the constraints and continued v1/v2 compatibility on PostgreSQL. Evaluate C6 (JSON/column ID consistency) separately. Evaluate C7 (keyed rate-window identifiers) as an operational privacy change, including old-key cutover and traffic from shared school connections. No schema change is required for the current summary contract. A new rating outcome or targeted retraction requires separate schema/contract review.
- Current live-service retention starts at collection and caps at the grant expiry. Grants last 30 days from issuance, codes 24 hours, and early issuance can truncate a nominal one-month pilot. Confirm cutoff and schedule with the lead. Report pending vs acknowledged withdrawal, make expiry during a long offline period visible, and ensure a restore cannot reintroduce withdrawn data to serving. Record each authorized storage/log/backup deadline in the protocol.

### Local recording lifecycle

| Action | Teacher view | On-device disposition | Network disposition |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ordinary delete before expiry | Immediately disappears from history, playback and reflections | Keep only required encrypted components and the deletion deadline for up to 15 days from deletion. The former 30-day deadline is superseded. Purge on time or on next available execution. | Keep the separately approved research rule for queued or acknowledged live summaries as a release decision. |
| Active recording reaches normal expiry | Disappears and is inaccessible on read | Purge under the existing 30-day active-recording policy. | Research expiry follows the approved summary contract. |
| Delete all data or research withdrawal | Any hidden recordings stay inaccessible | Bypass the 15-day window and immediately erase hidden recordings. Test crash recovery and explicit delete-all separately from withdrawal. | Cancel pending contribution and prioritize existing grant withdrawal where applicable. Reconcile remote acknowledgement when connectivity returns. |

In the ordinary-delete confirmation, explain the actual deletion date and that permanent local removal remains available through delete all data. For verification include a recording deleted shortly before the old 30-day boundary, a force-stopped phone past its deadline, and withdrawal while offline. Keep deletion metadata minimal and remove it when the lifecycle no longer needs it.

### Operational audit

Propose app-private audit entries and restricted backend events with UTC time, component, app/service version, action code, coarse state/outcome, retry count bucket, and a short-lived random operation reference where needed. Review required fields and retention with the research lead. Useful cases include route failure, capture interruption, corrupt storage, queue retry, remote acknowledgement and purge failure. Record audit actor/authority for privileged actions using an approved operational identifier. Never include raw headers, tokens, research record payloads, consent references, filename, contact details, or exception text that may embed teacher data. Keep the audit store bounded, and make incidental logging failure independent of capture and local recovery.

### PostHog usability events

Start with manually named steps such as `setup_completed`, `mic_check_result`, `record_attempt_result`, `review_opened`, `practice_chosen`, and `support_opened`. Use only approved enums and build versions. Keep card usefulness in the protocol's authorized outcome store and measure the rating step in PostHog only if approved, without sending the rating value. Disable session replay, autocapture, deep-link capture, automatic error/log uploads, push subscriptions and unnecessary remote config calls. Verify SDK default properties, IP handling, stable distinct IDs, offline queue contents and opt-out behavior on the wire. If the SDK cannot meet deletion or minimization requirements, use an approved buffered adapter with an exact schema. Use synthetic events to qualify PostHog. Keep telemetry from both adult rehearsal and live teachers local or disabled until an approved destination, consent, collection and retention policy exists. [PostHog Android SDK configuration](https://posthog.com/docs/libraries/android), [Android SDK reference](https://posthog.com/docs/libraries/android/reference).
